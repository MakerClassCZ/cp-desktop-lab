# The gamepad example in picogame

`examples/gamepad` again, drawn with `picogame`, CircuitPython's game engine: the D-pad flies a
ship over the stars with a flame behind it, A fires, and a shot that hits a rock breaks it into
sparks, with a sound for each. The top bar counts the rocks, the bottom one lists the buttons
held. `boards/files/picopad.toml` gives the virtual board the PicoPad's buttons and shows them on the
board panel.

From a release package:

    circuitpython --drive examples/picogame/CIRCUITPY --board-file boards/files/picopad.toml

From the port's directory after a build:

    build-linux/circuitpython --drive examples/picogame/CIRCUITPY --board-file boards/files/picopad.toml

- Click the panel's buttons, or use the keys: the arrows, A on `Z`, B on `X`, X on `A`, Y on `S`,
  START on Enter, SELECT on Backspace.
- A game controller plugged into the computer (Xbox, PlayStation, 8BitDo ...) presses the same
  buttons.

`code.py` follows the [picogame game-design skill](https://picogame.makerclass.cz/ai-agents/):
`picogame_game.setup()` takes over the display (the window's framebuffer here, the SPI display on
a PicoPad), `picogame_input.Buttons` reads the buttons, `picogame_clock.Clock` holds 30 frames a
second, the shapes are generated by `picogame_shapes`, the shots come from a `picogame_pool.Pool`,
the sounds are `picogame_sfx`'s, and the text is `terminalio.FONT` in two `picogame_ui.HudBar`s
outside the scene. The tuning constants at the top of `code.py` are its settings. `lib/` has
these modules from the [picogame helper libraries](https://github.com/MakerClassCZ/picogame-libs)
0.3.2, so the same `code.py` runs on a PicoPad.
